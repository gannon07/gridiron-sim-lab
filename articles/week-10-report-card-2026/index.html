Week 10 Report Card

We grade our own predictions every week. Week 10: last Thursday's start/sit post went 4 for 9, our worst week yet, even though the model's overall start/sit calls were right 66% of the time, its best mark this season. Here are the misses we own and the big games we didn't see coming.

Every week we grade our own predictions in public, good weeks and bad. This is the seventh week graded on numbers we posted before kickoff, and it covers every Week 10 game, Monday night's Chargers-Ravens game included. Chicago, Denver, Philadelphia and Tampa Bay were on bye.

All numbers below use half-PPR scoring and cover the players most people actually start: the top 24 quarterbacks, 36 running backs, 48 receivers and 18 tight ends by our projection.

The Short Version

Start/sit calls: 66% right. Take any two players at the same position. When the model ranked one above the other, he scored more 66% of the time. A coin flip gets you 50%. That's the best of our ten weeks so far, just ahead of last week's 65%.

Ranges: 71% of players finished inside the range we gave them. Every player gets a likely range, built to catch 8 of every 10 games, so 80% is the target. We fell short this week, our lowest mark of the season. The misses split evenly: 18 players finished above the top of their range and 18 below the bottom. Over all ten weeks we're at 79%, a little under target.

Last Thursday's Start/Sit Post Went 4 for 9

Last Thursday we named four players to start and five to sit, plus four hurt players to start if they were cleared. A start counts as right if he finished as a starter in a 12-team league, which means top 12 at quarterback and tight end, or top 24 at running back and receiver. A sit counts as right if he played and finished below that line. This was our worst start/sit week of the season.

The starts went 2 for 4. Trevor Lawrence was the best call. He threw for 352 yards and 3 touchdowns, with an interception, in the Jaguars' 40-23 win in Tennessee. He finished QB5 with 26.8 points. George Kittle caught 6 passes for 74 yards and finished TE7 with 10.4 points.

The other two starts missed. Corey Kiner got 10 carries for 25 yards and no catches in the Patriots' 35-10 loss to the Lions. He finished RB46 with 2.5 points. Keenan Allen caught 5 of 7 targets for 61 yards and finished WR39. The 49ers scored 46 points, but Christian McCaffrey and Deebo Samuel scored five of their touchdowns between them (more on both below).

The sits went 2 for 5. Justin Herbert threw for only 129 yards on Monday night. A rushing touchdown got him to 16.3 points, but he still finished QB14, just outside the starter line. Zay Flowers caught 6 passes for 74 yards but lost a fumble, and he finished WR41.

Three sits were wrong. Jayden Daniels threw for 249 yards and 3 touchdowns in Thursday night's 24-21 win over the Giants. Two interceptions held him to 20.8 points, but that was still good for QB8. Stefon Diggs caught 6 passes for 83 yards and a touchdown in the same game and finished WR9. Omarion Hampton ran 22 times for 105 yards and a touchdown in the Chargers' 23-20 win in Baltimore and finished RB11. The Chargers were tied for the second-lowest expected score of the week, and Hampton cleared the starter line anyway.

The four hurt players we said to start if cleared all played, and all four paid off. Deebo Samuel was cleared after missing three games with his hamstring injury and scored twice, once on a catch and once on a run. He finished WR5 with 24.4 points. Jared Goff threw for 303 yards and 3 touchdowns, with 2 interceptions, and finished QB10. Bhayshul Tuten ran 26 times for 88 yards and a touchdown and finished RB10. Garrett Wilson caught 7 passes for 106 yards and finished WR19, even with a lost fumble.

Tight ends were the weak spot. Their start/sit calls were barely better than a coin flip, and on average each one scored about 3 points less than we projected. Receivers missed their ranges most often: 16 of 48 finished outside them.

The Misses

Drake Maye, Patriots. Projected 18.9, scored 3.0. He hurt his ankle at the end of the opening drive in Munich and didn't return. He threw 4 passes. The bottom of his range was 6.7.

Tyler Warren, Colts. Projected 12.6, scored 1.4. He caught 2 of his 3 targets for 1 yard in the Colts' 24-22 win over Miami. The bottom of his range was 4.6.

Chris Olave, Saints. Projected 15.2, scored 4.5. He saw only 4 targets and caught 3 for 30 yards in a 20-17 loss to Carolina.

Kyren Williams (14.6 projected, 4.2 scored) ran 12 times for 23 yards in the Rams' 34-6 win in Arizona.

The Surprises

Christian McCaffrey, 49ers. Projected 16.0, scored 44.3, the biggest week of anyone we graded. He caught all 4 of his targets for 161 yards and 2 touchdowns and ran for another score. The top of his range was 27.9.

George Pickens, Cowboys. Projected 12.3, scored 38.0, in the same 46-40 shootout. He caught 11 of 18 targets for 145 yards and 3 touchdowns. Dak Prescott (18.4 projected, 34.4 scored) threw for 343 yards and 5 touchdowns in the loss.

Jahmyr Gibbs, Lions. Projected 21.0, scored 40.7. He ran for 153 yards and 2 touchdowns and caught 7 passes for 99 yards against the Patriots.

Patrick Mahomes (17.4 projected, 35.4 scored) threw for 348 yards and 5 touchdowns in the Chiefs' 35-13 win in Atlanta. Emmett Johnson (15.9 projected, 32.1 scored) ran for 101 yards and caught 2 touchdowns in the same game.
