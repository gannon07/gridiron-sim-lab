Week 11 Report Card

We grade our own predictions every week. Week 11: last Thursday's start/sit post went 4 for 9 for the second week in a row, while the model's overall start/sit calls were right 64% of the time and 79% of players finished inside their range. Here are the misses we own and the big games we didn't see coming.

Every week we grade our own predictions in public, good weeks and bad. This is the eighth week graded on numbers we posted before kickoff, and it covers every Week 11 game, Monday night's Bengals-Commanders game included. Atlanta, Cleveland, Green Bay, the Rams, New England and Seattle were on bye.

All numbers below use half-PPR scoring and cover the players most people actually start: the top 24 quarterbacks, 36 running backs, 48 receivers and 18 tight ends by our projection.

The Short Version

Start/sit calls: 64% right. Take any two players at the same position. When the model ranked one above the other, he scored more 64% of the time. A coin flip gets you 50%. That's a little below last week's 66%, our best so far, and right in line with the rest of the season.

Ranges: 79% of players finished inside the range we gave them. Every player gets a likely range, built to catch 8 of every 10 games, so 80% is the target. We landed just short, with 99 of 126 players inside. The misses leaned low this week: 19 players finished below the bottom of their range and only 8 above the top. Over all eleven weeks we're at 79%.

Last Thursday's Start/Sit Post Went 4 for 9

Last Thursday we named four players to start and five to sit, plus four hurt players to start if they were cleared. A start counts as right if he finished as a starter in a 12-team league, which means top 12 at quarterback and tight end, or top 24 at running back and receiver. A sit counts as right if he played and finished below that line. That's 4 for 9 for the second week in a row, tied for our worst.

The starts went 2 for 4, and both hits came from Thursday night's 26-3 Texans win over the Colts. C.J. Stroud completed 21 of 27 passes for 194 yards and 3 touchdowns, with an interception. He finished QB7 with 20.5 points. David Montgomery ran 26 times for 107 yards and caught 3 passes. He finished RB13 with 13.9 points.

The other two starts missed. Rome Odunze caught all 4 of his targets for 45 yards and finished WR45. Chicago was expected to score about 30 points and scored 21. Dalton Schultz caught 1 pass for 11 yards in that same Texans win. He finished TE41 with 1.6 points.

The sits went 2 for 5. Dallas Goedert caught 1 pass for 38 yards in the Eagles' 19-16 win over Pittsburgh and finished TE21. Alvin Kamara touched the ball twice, but one was a 1-yard touchdown catch. That gave him 6.7 points, and he still finished RB27, below the starter line.

Three sits were wrong. Jared Goff threw for 279 yards and 2 touchdowns with no interceptions in the Lions' 31-21 win over Tampa Bay. He finished QB8 with 20.4 points. Jameson Williams caught 4 passes for 93 yards and a touchdown in the same game and finished WR8. Ollie Gordon II ran 11 times for 69 yards in Miami's 26-9 loss in Buffalo. His 7.7 points put him at RB24, the last starter spot, so that call counts as a miss by the smallest possible margin.

Of the four hurt players we said to start if cleared, Brock Bowers was ruled out with his ankle and thigh injuries, so he doesn't count. The other three played, and two paid off. Carnell Tate caught 2 passes for 58 yards and a touchdown in Dallas and finished WR19. T.J. Hockenson caught 6 passes for 55 yards and finished TE10. Bhayshul Tuten missed: 10 carries for 33 yards and 3 catches for 20 yards left him at RB26.

Quarterbacks were the easiest to pin down this week. Twenty-two of 24 finished inside their range, and the two who didn't both scored more than we expected.

Tight ends were the weak spot for ranges. Seven of 18 finished outside them, six of those on the low side, and on average each one scored about 2 points less than we projected. Receivers had the weakest start/sit calls of any position, right 60% of the time.

The Misses

Ja'Marr Chase, Bengals. Projected 18.4, scored 5.6. He saw 9 targets on Monday night but caught 5 for 31 yards in a 22-20 loss in Washington. The bottom of his range was 6.4.

J.K. Dobbins, Broncos. Projected 11.1, scored 0.0. He had one carry for no yards in Denver's 38-24 win over the Raiders. Rookie Jonah Coleman got 24 carries in the same game (more on him below).

Chase Brown, Bengals. Projected 16.1, scored 6.1. He ran 6 times for 6 yards and caught 5 passes for 30 yards in the same Monday night loss.

Two Bears missed in Chicago's 21-13 win over the Saints. Luther Burden III (11.8 projected) scored 2.3 and Kyle Monangai (about 11 projected) scored 1.8. DK Metcalf (9.7 projected) caught 1 pass for 2 yards in Pittsburgh's loss in Philadelphia.

The Surprises

Jonah Coleman, Broncos. Projected 7.8, scored 32.9, the biggest week of any running back. He ran 24 times for 111 yards and 3 touchdowns and caught 4 passes. The top of his range was 15.7.

Fernando Mendoza, Raiders. Projected 7.7, scored 22.9 in the same game. In his second start, the rookie threw for 267 yards and 3 touchdowns, with an interception, and finished QB3. Ashton Jeanty (12.5 projected, 25.4 scored) added 79 rushing yards and 5 catches for 90 yards and a touchdown.

Dak Prescott, Cowboys. Projected 21.3, scored 35.9, the best quarterback week of Week 11. He completed 28 of 33 passes for 325 yards and 4 touchdowns and ran for a fifth in a 51-30 win over Tennessee.

Travis Kelce (9.3 projected, 22.4 scored) caught 6 passes for 74 yards and 2 touchdowns in the Chiefs' 34-7 win over Arizona. Mike Evans (7.5 projected, 19.9 scored) caught 6 passes for 109 yards and a touchdown in the 49ers' 35-10 win over Minnesota.
