Week 9 Report Card

We grade our own predictions every week. Week 9: last Thursday's start/sit post went 6 for 9, and the model's overall start/sit calls were right 65% of the time, our best week this season. Here are the biggest misses and surprises, including the Jordan Love call we got wrong.

Every week we grade our own predictions in public, good weeks and bad. This is the sixth week graded on numbers we posted before kickoff, and it covers every Week 9 game, Monday night's Bills-Vikings game included. Pittsburgh and Tennessee were on bye.

All numbers below use half-PPR scoring and cover the players most people actually start: the top 24 quarterbacks, 36 running backs, 48 receivers and 18 tight ends by our projection.

The Short Version

Start/sit calls: 65% right. Take any two players at the same position. When the model ranked one above the other, he scored more 65% of the time. A coin flip gets you 50%. It's the best of our nine weeks so far, just ahead of Weeks 3 and 7.

Ranges: 83% of players finished inside the range we gave them. Every player gets a likely range, built to catch 8 of every 10 games, so 80% is the target. We beat it this week, and over all nine weeks we're at 80%, right on target. Every miss below finished under the bottom of his range, and every surprise finished above the top of his.

Last Thursday's Start/Sit Post Went 6 for 9

Last Thursday we named four players to start and five to sit, plus three hurt running backs to start if they were cleared. A start counts as right if he finished as a starter in a 12-team league, which means top 12 at quarterback and tight end, or top 24 at running back and receiver. A sit counts as right if he played and finished below that line.

The starts went 2 for 4. CeeDee Lamb was the best call of the week. He caught 10 of 16 targets for 195 yards and a touchdown and finished WR1 with 28.5 points, even with a lost fumble. Brock Purdy threw for 232 yards and 2 touchdowns and ran for another in the 38-17 win over the Raiders. He finished QB4 with 25.3 points.

Both running back starts missed, and they missed badly. D'Andre Swift ran 12 times for 31 yards and didn't catch his only target. He finished RB51 with 3.1 points, even with Kyle Monangai inactive again. Tyler Allgeier got the work we expected, 17 carries, but they went for 46 yards in a 17-3 loss at Seattle. He finished RB37.

The sits went 4 for 5. Zay Flowers caught 3 passes for 6 yards in Thursday's 33-10 loss to the Jaguars and finished WR63. Justin Herbert threw for 193 yards with a touchdown and an interception in a 44-22 loss to the Texans and finished QB21. T.J. Hockenson caught 4 passes for 33 yards on Monday night and finished TE22. Dallas Goedert caught 5 passes for 50 yards and finished TE14, just outside the starter line.

Jordan Love is the one we got wrong, and it wasn't close. He went 23 of 29 for 372 yards and 3 touchdowns and ran in a fourth. He finished QB1 with 36.2 points in the Packers' 45-27 win in New England.

Two of the three hurt running backs played. Christian McCaffrey paid off. He scored twice, once running and once catching, and finished RB4 with 21.7 points. Saquon Barkley scored a touchdown but ran 13 times for only 22 yards and finished RB25, one spot short of the starter line, so that one's a miss. Cam Skattebo was inactive with his hamstring, so he isn't graded.

Tight ends and receivers carried the week. Their start/sit calls were right 68% of the time, and 17 of the 18 tight ends finished inside their range.

Running backs were the weak spot again. Only 25 of 36 finished inside their range, and on average each one scored about 3 points less than we projected.

The Misses

D'Andre Swift, Bears. Projected 17.9, scored 3.1. The biggest miss of the week was one of our own start calls. The bottom of his range was 6.7.

Jared Goff, Lions. Projected 18.1, scored 5.3. He threw for 170 yards with no touchdowns and an interception in a 19-9 win at Miami. On the other side, Malik Willis (14.2 projected, 3.8 scored) threw for 128 yards and an interception.

Devin Singletary, Giants. Projected 15.4, scored 4.9. He got 21 carries with Skattebo out, but they went for 31 yards, even in a 19-11 win in Philadelphia.

Ashton Jeanty (14.7 projected, 4.5 scored) had 6 carries for 25 yards and 5 catches for minus 5 yards in the Raiders' loss at San Francisco.

The Surprises

Jordan Love, Packers. Projected 15.1, scored 36.2, the biggest week of anyone we graded. The top of his range was 25.2. Two of his receivers beat their ranges too. Matthew Golden (11.3 projected, 27.4 scored) caught all 10 of his targets for 164 yards and a touchdown, and Christian Watson (11.0 projected, 26.6 scored) caught 7 passes for 171 yards and a touchdown.

C.J. Stroud, Texans. Projected 16.1, scored 35.4. He threw for 384 yards and 5 touchdowns, with 1 interception, in the 44-22 win over the Chargers. The top of his range was 26.4.

Kenneth Walker III, Chiefs. Projected 19.5, scored 34.3. He ran for 102 yards and a touchdown and caught 4 passes for 41 yards and 2 more in a 35-12 win over the Jets.

Bucky Irving (13.6 projected, 26.5 scored) ran for 97 yards and 2 touchdowns in the Buccaneers' 27-24 loss in Chicago, the same game where Swift came up empty.
