Who's Getting More of the Ball, and Who's Losing It

Six players whose workload has changed a lot since Week 5, led by Tony Pollard and Ashton Jeanty, and how far our model has moved with them.

Fantasy points follow the ball. A running back who goes from 14 carries a game to 22 is a different player, even if his name and team are the same. Nine weeks in, a handful of roles have changed a lot, and some of those changes are bigger than the rankings show.

Everything here is half PPR for a 12-team league, from Wednesday morning's update.

The Short Version

Tony Pollard and Woody Marks are getting far more carries because a teammate got hurt. Matthew Golden and Colston Loveland are seeing more targets, and their jumps don't trace back to one teammate's injury the way Pollard's and Marks's do. Ashton Jeanty and Juwan Johnson have lost work, and our model still projects both of them closer to their early-season roles. We think that's too high for both.

How We Measured It

We split the season in two: Weeks 1 to 5, and Weeks 6 to 9. For each player we counted carries and targets per game he played in each half, plus how often he was on the field. The numbers come from the nflverse game stats and snap counts.

Our model builds each player's workload from his whole season and last season, so a change over one month only moves it part of the way. That keeps one odd game from swinging a ranking. It also means the model is slow to catch up when a role really changes. That's where this list helps.

Getting More Work

Player Team Weeks 1-5 Weeks 6-9 Rest of season
Tony Pollard Titans 13.6 carries 21.7 carries RB16
Woody Marks Texans 8.6 carries 17.3 carries RB31
Matthew Golden Packers 8.8 targets 12.0 targets WR9
Colston Loveland Bears 4.6 targets 8.5 targets TE5

Tony Pollard, Titans. He was on the field for about half the plays early in the season. Since Week 6 it's been 78%, with 19, 25 and 21 carries. Tyjae Spears has a knee injury and one carry since Week 5, so the work has gone to Pollard. Our model gives Pollard 17.5 carries against Jacksonville this week and ranks him RB16 for the week and the rest of the season. If Spears comes back, Pollard's share could drop back toward where it started.

Woody Marks, Texans. He went from about 9 carries a game to about 17 once David Montgomery hurt his groin. Montgomery has missed three straight games, and his coach hopes he can return to practice this week or next. Our model gives Marks 12.9 carries this week, because it counts his slow start and the chance Montgomery returns. He's RB23 at Cleveland and a decent flex play while Montgomery is out. Forbes expects the two to split the work once Montgomery is back.

Matthew Golden, Packers. He's played about the same share of snaps all season, but his targets went from 8.8 a game to 12.0, with 10 or more in each of the last four games. Jayden Reed's season ended in September, before this jump started. He kept it up while playing through shoulder and foot injuries late in October. Our model projects 9.8 targets a game, so if the last month is the real Golden, he's better than his WR9 rank. He's WR10 this week against Minnesota.

Colston Loveland, Bears. His targets nearly doubled, from 4.6 a game to 8.5. They were already rising before Cole Kmet's season-ending knee injury in Week 7, with 7 and 12 in Weeks 6 and 7. Our model projects about 6 a game from here and ranks him TE5 for the rest of the season. The Bears are on bye this week, so this is about next week and beyond. If you're thin at tight end, he's a strong player to target in a trade.

Losing Work

Player Team Weeks 1-5 Weeks 6-9 Rest of season
Ashton Jeanty Raiders 17.6 carries 6.5 carries RB11
Juwan Johnson Saints 6.4 targets 2.3 targets TE14

Ashton Jeanty, Raiders. This is the biggest change on the list. He had 23, 21 and 19 carries in the first three games, then 10, 8, 2 and 6. Rookie Mike Washington Jr. made his first start in Week 8 and ran 25 times. In Week 9 at San Francisco, both backs had six carries and Jeanty played more snaps as the passing-down back. His targets haven't changed, about 6 a game.

Our model still gives Jeanty 13.3 carries this week and ranks him RB11 for the rest of the season. That's the gap we'd worry about most. The targets give him a floor, but if Washington keeps the early-down work, our number for Jeanty is too high. Don't trade for him at an RB1 price.

Juwan Johnson, Saints. He had 6.4 targets a game through Week 5 and 2.3 since, while his snap share fell from 68% to 56%. We found no injury report behind it. A Saints site called it a role that has vanished with no explanation. Our model still projects 5.6 targets a game and ranks him TE8 this week against Carolina. We'd treat that as optimistic until he gets more looks.

What to Do With This

Golden's and Loveland's gains don't hinge on a teammate staying hurt, so they're the safer ones to pay for in a trade, while Pollard's and Marks's extra work may end when a teammate gets healthy. When a role shrinks the way Jeanty's has, trust the recent games over the ranking.
